# 7. Empirical Analytical Findings Across the Tripartite Suite

> [!abstract] Executive Summary of Findings
> Programmatic analysis of the 12,543 records across the three primary PwC Switzerland datasets and four auxiliary Backing lookup tables revealed significant operational bottlenecks, commercial vulnerabilities, and organizational equity imbalances. This document details the grounded findings across all three client divisions.

---

## 📞 Domain 1: Call Centre Operations & Telephony SLAs (Claire)

### Finding 1.1: Severe Inbound Queue Abandonment (18.92%)
- Out of 5,000 incoming calls in Q1 2021, **946 callers (18.92%) abandoned the queue** before reaching an agent.
- The average speed of answer (ASA) for connected calls is **67.52 seconds**. Callers who abandoned faced queue wait times exceeding 75–120 seconds during peak hours.
- Abandonment is concentrated during the midday peak (**11:00 AM – 2:00 PM**), where queue arrival volume surges by **42%** above the daily baseline.

### Finding 1.2: High Problem Resolution on Connected Calls (89.94%)
- Once a caller connects with an agent, the First-Contact Resolution (FCR) rate is exceptionally high (**89.94%** of answered calls, 3,646 resolved).
- **Core Diagnosis**: Customer dissatisfaction is overwhelmingly driven by **queue wait times and accessibility bottlenecks**, not by agent technical incompetence or procedural failure.

### Finding 1.3: Balanced Demand Distribution Across Inquiry Topics
- Inbound inquiries are distributed almost uniformly across 5 core topics:
  1. `Streaming`: 1,019 calls (20.38%)
  2. `Payment related`: 1,007 calls (20.14%)
  3. `Technical Support`: 1,004 calls (20.08%)
  4. `Admin Support`: 994 calls (19.88%)
  5. `Contract related`: 976 calls (19.52%)
- Technical Support inquiries exhibit the lowest average CSAT (3.32) and longest handle times due to complex troubleshooting protocols.

### Finding 1.4: The Agent Performance Quadrant (Talk Duration vs Calls Answered)
Evaluating the 8 agents across the 2D matrix (**Average Handle Time / Talk Duration vs Calls Answered**) reveals four distinct operational archetypes:
1. **High-Volume Workhorses (Jim & Dan)**:
   - **Jim**: 536 answered calls (team maximum), 90.49% resolution rate, 3.41 CSAT.
   - **Dan**: 523 answered calls, 90.06% resolution rate, 3.45 CSAT.
2. **Speed & Queue Relief Specialist (Becky)**:
   - Lowest ASA on the team (**65.33 seconds**) and rapid handle time (219 seconds), providing vital queue pressure relief during midday arrival surges.
3. **Thorough Customer Satisfaction Leader (Martha)**:
   - Martha logged the longest talk duration (238 seconds) but achieved the highest CSAT rating on the team (**3.47 / 5.00**), proving that customer satisfaction in technical topics rewards thoroughness.
4. **Coaching & SLA Priority (Joe)**:
   - Lowest call volume (484 answered), slowest speed of answer (**70.99 seconds**), and lowest CSAT rating (**3.33 / 5.00**), representing the primary candidate for targeted coaching.

## 🔄 Domain 2: Customer Retention & Revenue Protection (David Chen)

### Finding 2.1: $139K Monthly Recurring Revenue Lost to Churn
- Overall customer churn stands at **26.54%** (1,869 churned accounts out of 7,043).
- Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) lost to churn equals **$139,130.85 per month** ($1.67M annualized revenue leakage).
- Average tenure of churned customers is only **17.98 months**, compared to **37.57 months** for retained subscribers, indicating that customer attrition occurs early in the lifecycle.

### Finding 2.2: Extreme Contract Horizon Elasticity
Churn rates vary dramatically by contract commitment:
- **Month-to-Month Contracts**: **42.71% churn rate** (1,655 churned out of 3,875 accounts). Accounts on month-to-month contracts represent **88.55% of all churned subscribers**!
- **One-Year Contracts**: **11.27% churn rate** (166 churned out of 1,473 accounts).
- **Two-Year Contracts**: **2.83% churn rate** (48 churned out of 1,695 accounts).
- **Takeaway**: Converting month-to-month subscribers to annual agreements is the single highest-leverage retention strategy.

### Finding 2.3: Fiber Optic Dissatisfaction & High-Charges Paradox
- Subscribers with **Fiber Optic** internet service churn at **41.89%** (1,297 churned / 3,096), compared to **18.96%** for DSL and **7.40%** for customers without internet service.
- Churned customers had an average monthly charge of **$74.44**, compared to **$61.27** for retained customers. High monthly bills paired with technical service instability create severe customer churn vulnerability.

### Finding 2.4: Technical Support Tickets as the #1 Churn Precursor
- Retained customers average only **0.28 technical support tickets**, whereas churned customers average **0.86 technical support tickets**.
- Customers who open **$\ge 2$ technical trouble tickets** exhibit a churn rate of **63.4%** ($2.4\times$ the baseline rate), demonstrating a direct operational bridge between Call Center technical support interactions (Domain 1) and contract termination (Domain 2).

### Finding 2.5: Payment Method Friction
- Customers paying via **Electronic Check** experience a **45.29% churn rate** (1,071 churned / 2,365), compared to **15.24%** for automatic credit card deductions and **16.71%** for automatic bank transfers.

## 👥 Domain 3: Diversity, Equity & Human Capital Governance (HR Leadership)

### Finding 3.1: The Executive "Glass Ceiling" in Senior Leadership
While female employees represent **41.0%** of the general workforce (205 out of 500 employees), female representation diminishes sharply at senior hierarchical tiers:
- **Job Level 6 (Junior Officer)**: 50.56% Female (91 of 180).
- **Job Level 5 (Senior Officer)**: 42.22% Female (57 of 135).
- **Job Level 4 (Manager)**: 37.50% Female (45 of 120).
- **Job Level 3 (Senior Manager)**: 21.05% Female (8 of 38).
- **Job Level 2 (Director)**: 15.79% Female (3 of 19).
- **Job Level 1 (Executive Director)**: **12.50% Female (1 of 8)**.
- **Combined Executive Tiers (Levels 1 & 2)**: Only **14.81% Female** (4 of 27 leaders).

### Finding 3.2: FY21 Promotion Velocity Disparity
- Across the entire organization, 51 employees were promoted in FY21 (10.20% overall promotion rate).
- However, the promotion velocity exhibits a clear gender gap:
  - **Male Promotion Rate**: **11.19%** (33 of 295 men promoted).
  - **Female Promotion Rate**: **8.78%** (18 of 205 women promoted).
- Controlling for performance ratings: Among employees who received the top performance rating of 4 in FY20, **22.5% of men were promoted**, compared to only **14.3% of women**.

### Finding 3.3: Turnover Distribution & Attrition Vulnerability
- Corporate turnover in FY20 was **9.40%** (47 leavers out of 500 personnel).
- Leavers are heavily concentrated in junior operational grades: **26 leavers in Job Level 6** and **14 leavers in Job Level 5**, representing **85.1% of all organizational departures**.
- Female turnover in junior grades (11.2%) exceeds male turnover (8.1%), reflecting career stagnation and lack of visible advancement pathways.

### Finding 3.4: Performance Review Assessment (PRA) Equity Disparities (`Backing 4`)
- Cross-referencing `Fact_Employees` with `Dim_PRA_Equity` (`Backing 4`) reveals that overall Job Level `3 - Senior Manager` is the **only organizational tier flagged as `Uneven - Men benefit`**.
- Specifically, within `Sales & Marketing` (17 staff) and `Internal Services` (10 staff), promotion and appraisal calibration committees disproportionately favored male candidates for Senior Manager appointments.
- In contrast, Job Levels 2 (Director), 4 (Manager), 5 (Senior Officer), and 6 (Junior Officer) were classified as statistically **`Even`**.

### Finding 3.5: Time-in-Grade Promotion Stagnation (`Backing 1`)
- Granular employee census data from `Dim_EmployeeCensus` (`Backing 1`) demonstrates that female Senior Officers (Job Level 5) average **3.8 years in grade** before promotion review, compared to **2.4 years** for male colleagues with identical performance ratings, highlighting an unmonitored promotion latency penalty.

### Finding 3.6: Geographic & Executive Nationality Clustering (`Backing 3`)
- While domestic Swiss nationals represent **44.80%** of total personnel (224 of 500 in `Dim_NationalityCensus`), they hold **62.50%** of Executive Director roles (5 of 8), with expatriate European talent primarily clustered in Junior Officer and Specialist ranks.
